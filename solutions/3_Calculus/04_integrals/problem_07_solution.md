# Exercise 7: Integration by Parts as Reversing the Product Rule

## 1. Problem Statement

Compute
$$\int xe^x\,dx.$$
First write the product differentiation rule and, on that basis, explain the meaning of the integration-by-parts formula. Verify the result by differentiation.

---

## 2. Theoretical Background and Concepts

- **Product Rule for Differentiation:** For two differentiable functions $u(x)$ and $v(x)$, the derivative of their product is
  $$\frac{d}{dx}\left[u(x)v(x)\right] = u'(x)v(x) + u(x)v'(x).$$
- **Derivation and Meaning of Integration by Parts:**
  Integrating both sides of the product rule with respect to $x$ gives:
  $$\int \frac{d}{dx}\left[u(x)v(x)\right]\,dx = \int u'(x)v(x)\,dx + \int u(x)v'(x)\,dx,$$
  $$u(x)v(x) = \int u'(x)v(x)\,dx + \int u(x)v'(x)\,dx.$$
  Rearranging to isolate $\int u(x)v'(x)\,dx$ yields the **integration-by-parts formula**:
  $$\int u(x)v'(x)\,dx = u(x)v(x) - \int u'(x)v(x)\,dx,$$
  or in differential notation ($du = u'(x)\,dx$, $dv = v'(x)\,dx$):
  $$\int u\,dv = uv - \int v\,du.$$
  **Meaning:** Integration by parts trades an integral of the product $u\,dv$ for the boundary product $uv$ minus a new integral $\int v\,du$ in which $u$ has been differentiated to $du$ and $dv$ has been integrated to $v$. It succeeds when $\int v\,du$ is simpler than $\int u\,dv$.

---

## 3. Detailed Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Choosing the Factors $u$ and $dv$
In $\int xe^x\,dx$, the integrand is a product of a polynomial $x$ and an exponential $e^x$.
- If we differentiate $x$, it simplifies to the constant $1$.
- If we integrate $e^x$, it remains $e^x$ (no increase in complexity).

Therefore, we choose:
$$\begin{aligned}
u(x) &= x & \implies \quad u'(x) &= 1 \quad (du = dx), \\
v'(x) &= e^x & \implies \quad v(x) &= e^x \quad (dv = e^x\,dx).
\end{aligned}$$

### Step 2: Applying the Integration-by-Parts Formula
Substituting $u, v, du, dv$ into $\int u\,dv = uv - \int v\,du$:
$$\begin{aligned}
\int xe^x\,dx &= x \cdot e^x - \int e^x \cdot 1\,dx \\
&= xe^x - \int e^x\,dx \\
&= xe^x - e^x + C \\
&= (x - 1)e^x + C, \quad C \in \mathbb{R}.
\end{aligned}$$

---

## 4. Verification and Consistency Checks

We differentiate $F(x) = xe^x - e^x + C$ using the product rule on the first term $xe^x$:
$$\begin{aligned}
F'(x) &= \frac{d}{dx}(xe^x) - \frac{d}{dx}(e^x) + \frac{d}{dx}(C) \\
&= \left[(x)'e^x + x(e^x)'\right] - e^x + 0 \\
&= \left(1 \cdot e^x + xe^x\right) - e^x \\
&= xe^x.
\end{aligned}$$
Notice how the $-e^x$ term in the antiderivative specifically cancels the extra $+e^x$ term generated by the product rule when differentiating $xe^x$. This confirms our result.
