# Exercise 14: Cone and Cylinder

## 1. Problem Statement
Compare
$$x^2+y^2=z^2$$
and
$$x^2+y^2=4.$$
For each surface, describe the cross-sections $z=c$, $x=0$, and $y=0$, make a sketch, and explain why the radius of the cross-section depends on $z$ in the first case but is constant in the second.

## 2. Theoretical Background and Concepts
- **Right Circular Cone ($x^2 + y^2 = a^2 z^2$):** A homogeneous second-degree equation where all three variables are present and $z^2$ equals a positive multiple of $x^2 + y^2$. Horizontal slices grow linearly with $|z|$, and vertical slices through the axis are pairs of intersecting lines.
- **Right Circular Cylinder ($x^2 + y^2 = R^2$):** A surface in $\mathbb{R}^3$ whose equation lacks the variable $z$. Whenever a coordinate variable is missing from a 3D equation $F(x,y) = 0$, the surface is a **cylinder** with rulings (generators) parallel to the axis of the missing variable ($Oz$).

## 3. Detailed Step-by-Step Solution

### Surface 1: Circular Cone ($x^2 + y^2 = z^2$)

1. **Horizontal cross-sections $z = c$:**
   Substitute $z = c$:
   $$x^2 + y^2 = c^2.$$
   - For $c = 0$: $x^2 + y^2 = 0 \implies (0,0,0)$, a **single point** (the apex/vertex of the cone).
   - For $c \neq 0$: A **circle** centered at $(0, 0, c)$ with radius $r(c) = \sqrt{c^2} = |c|$, which increases linearly with distance $|c|$ from the $xy$-plane.

2. **Vertical cross-section $x = 0$ (in the $yz$-plane):**
   $$0^2 + y^2 = z^2 \implies y^2 = z^2 \implies z = \pm y.$$
   This is a **pair of intersecting straight lines** $z = y$ and $z = -y$ meeting at the origin $(0,0,0)$ at a $90^\circ$ angle.

3. **Vertical cross-section $y = 0$ (in the $xz$-plane):**
   $$x^2 + 0^2 = z^2 \implies x^2 = z^2 \implies z = \pm x.$$
   This is a **pair of intersecting straight lines** $z = x$ and $z = -x$ meeting at the origin $(0,0,0)$.

---

### Surface 2: Circular Cylinder ($x^2 + y^2 = 4$)

1. **Horizontal cross-sections $z = c$:**
   Since $z$ does not appear in the equation $x^2 + y^2 = 4$, for **every** $c \in \mathbb{R}$, the cross-section in the plane $z = c$ has the same equation:
   $$x^2 + y^2 = 4 = 2^2, \quad z = c.$$
   This is a **circle** centered at $(0, 0, c)$ with constant radius $r = 2$.

2. **Vertical cross-section $x = 0$ (in the $yz$-plane):**
   $$0^2 + y^2 = 4 \implies y^2 = 4 \implies y = \pm 2.$$
   In the $yz$-plane ($x = 0$), where $z \in \mathbb{R}$ is arbitrary, the equations $y = 2$ and $y = -2$ represent **two parallel vertical straight lines** at distance $2$ from the $Oz$ axis.

3. **Vertical cross-section $y = 0$ (in the $xz$-plane):**
   $$x^2 + 0^2 = 4 \implies x^2 = 4 \implies x = \pm 2.$$
   In the $xz$-plane ($y = 0$), the equations $x = 2$ and $x = -2$ represent **two parallel vertical straight lines** at distance $2$ from the $Oz$ axis.

### Why the Cross-Section Radius Depends on $z$ for the Cone but Is Constant for the Cylinder
- **Algebraic explanation:**
  In a horizontal plane $z = c$, the distance $r$ of a point $(x, y, c)$ from the $Oz$ axis satisfies $r^2 = x^2 + y^2$.
  - In the first equation ($x^2 + y^2 = z^2$), the right-hand side explicitly contains $z^2$. Setting $z = c$ binds the radius squared to the height: $r^2 = c^2 \implies r = |c|$.
  - In the second equation ($x^2 + y^2 = 4$), the variable $z$ is **completely absent** from the equation (its coefficient is $0 \cdot z^2 + 0 \cdot z = 0$). Consequently, $z$ is a free variable: a point $(x, y, z)$ lies on the surface if and only if $(x, y)$ lies on the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 4$, regardless of the value of $z$. Hence $r = 2$ for all $z$.
- **Geometric explanation:**
  The cone is generated by rotating the inclined line $z = x$ around the $Oz$ axis, so its distance from the axis grows linearly with height $|z|$. The cylinder is generated by translating the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ vertically along the $Oz$ axis (or rotating the vertical line $x = 2$ around $Oz$), keeping the distance from the axis fixed at $2$.

## 4. Verification and Consistency Checks
- **Test points at heights $z = 0$ and $z = 2$:**
  - At $z = 0$: On the cone, only $(0,0,0)$ satisfies $x^2+y^2=0$; on the cylinder, $(2,0,0)$ satisfies $2^2+0^2=4$.
  - At $z = 2$: Both surfaces have the same horizontal cross-section $x^2 + y^2 = 4, \, z = 2$! Thus, the cone $x^2 + y^2 = z^2$ and the cylinder $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ intersect along the two circles $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ at heights $z = 2$ and $z = -2$.
