# Exercise 15: Recognition and Sketch Without a Given Name

## 1. Problem Statement
Consider the two equations:

$$4x^2+9y^2-8x+36y+4=0,$$

$$4x^2+y^2=z^2.$$

For the first equation, reduce it to canonical form, recognize the curve, give its most important features, and make a sketch.

For the second equation, do not begin by using a ready-made name for the surface. First describe the cross-sections $z=c$ and the cross-sections by the planes $x=0$ and $y=0$, paying attention to the different scales along the axes. Only then identify the surface and make a sketch.

## 2. Theoretical Background and Concepts
- **Part 1 (Completing the Square for 2D Conics):** Given $Ax^2 + Cy^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0$ with $A, C > 0$, we factor out $A$ and $C$ and complete the square in $x$ and $y$ to arrive at the canonical form of a shifted ellipse:
  $$\frac{(x - x_0)^2}{a^2} + \frac{(y - y_0)^2}{b^2} = 1.$$
- **Part 2 (Cross-Section Analysis of 3D Surfaces):** By slicing a 3D equation $F(x,y,z) = 0$ with coordinate planes ($x = 0$, $y = 0$) and horizontal planes ($z = c$), we deduce the 3D geometry directly from 2D curves (lines, circles, ellipses) and their scaling with $c$.

## 3. Detailed Step-by-Step Solution

### Part 1: Analysis of $4x^2 + 9y^2 - 8x + 36y + 4 = 0$

#### Step 1.1: Reduce to canonical form
Group the $x$-terms and $y$-terms and move the constant term to the right-hand side:
$$(4x^2 - 8x) + (9y^2 + 36y) = -4.$$
Factor out $4$ from the $x$-terms and $9$ from the $y$-terms:
$$4(x^2 - 2x) + 9(y^2 + 4y) = -4.$$
Complete the square inside each parenthesis:
- $x^2 - 2x = (x - 1)^2 - 1$,
- $y^2 + 4y = (y + 2)^2 - 4$.

Substitute these back into the equation:
$$4\left[(x - 1)^2 - 1\right] + 9\left[(y + 2)^2 - 4\right] = -4$$
$$4(x - 1)^2 - 4 + 9(y + 2)^2 - 36 = -4$$
$$4(x - 1)^2 + 9(y + 2)^2 = 36.$$
Divide both sides by $36$:
$$\frac{(x - 1)^2}{9} + \frac{(y + 2)^2}{4} = 1.$$
This is the **canonical form** of the curve.

#### Step 1.2: Recognize the curve and list its features
- **Type of curve:** **Shifted Ellipse** (stretched horizontally along the direction of the $x$-axis).
- **Center:** $S = (1, -2)$.
- **Semiaxis lengths:**
  - Horizontal semiaxis (semi-major axis): $a = \sqrt{9} = 3$.
  - Vertical semiaxis (semi-minor axis): $b = \sqrt{4} = 2$.
- **Vertices:**
  - Horizontal vertices: $(1 \pm 3, -2) \implies (-2, -2)$ and $(4, -2)$.
  - Vertical vertices: $(1, -2 \pm 2) \implies (1, 0)$ (tangent to the $x$-axis at $(1,0)$!) and $(1, -4)$.
- **Foci:** $c = \sqrt{a^2 - b^2} = \sqrt{9 - 4} = \sqrt{5}$, located at $(1 \pm \sqrt{5}, -2)$.

#### Step 1.3: Sketch of the shifted ellipse
```text

----------+----*----+---------> x
    . - - | - ' ' - | - - .
  /       |         |       \
```

### Part 2: Analysis of $4x^2 + y^2 = z^2$ via Cross-Sections

#### Step 2.1: Horizontal cross-sections $z = c$
Substitute $z = c$ into $4x^2 + y^2 = z^2$:
$$4x^2 + y^2 = c^2.$$
- **For $c = 0$:** $4x^2 + y^2 = 0 \implies x = 0, \, y = 0$. The cross-section is a **single point**, the origin $(0, 0, 0)$.
- **For $c \neq 0$:** Divide both sides by $c^2 > 0$:
  $$\frac{4x^2}{c^2} + \frac{y^2}{c^2} = 1 \implies \frac{x^2}{(c/2)^2} + \frac{y^2}{c^2} = 1.$$
  In each plane $z = c \neq 0$, the cross-section is an **ellipse** centered at $(0, 0, c)$ with:
  - semiaxis along the $x$-axis: $a(c) = \frac{|c|}{2}$,
  - semiaxis along the $y$-axis: $b(c) = |c|$.
  **Scale comparison:** At every non-zero height $z = c$, the ellipse is **twice as wide along the $y$-axis as along the $x$-axis** ($b(c) = 2a(c)$), and both semiaxes grow linearly in direct proportion to $|c|$.

#### Step 2.2: Vertical cross-sections by $x = 0$ and $y = 0$
- **Plane $x = 0$ ($yz$-plane):**
  Setting $x = 0$ gives:
  $$y^2 = z^2 \implies z = \pm y.$$
  This is a **pair of intersecting straight lines** through $(0,0,0)$ with slopes $\pm 1$ with respect to the $y$-axis (making a $45^\circ$ angle with the $y$-axis and $Oz$ axis).

- **Plane $y = 0$ ($xz$-plane):**
  Setting $y = 0$ gives:
  $$4x^2 = z^2 \implies z = \pm 2x.$$
  This is a **pair of intersecting straight lines** through $(0,0,0)$ with steeper slopes $\pm 2$ with respect to the $x$-axis (for a given height $z$, $|x| = \frac{1}{2}|z|$, so the lines stay closer to the $Oz$ axis in the $xz$-plane than in the $yz$-plane).

#### Step 2.3: Identification of the surface and sketch
Combining these observations:
- The surface passes through a single apex at $(0,0,0)$, is generated by straight lines passing through the origin ($z = \pm 2x$ in $y=0$, $z = \pm y$ in $x=0$), and has horizontal cross-sections that are homothetic ellipses expanding linearly with $|z|$.
- Therefore, the surface is a **Double Elliptic Cone** (with vertex at the origin $(0,0,0)$ and axis along $Oz$, stretched twice as much in the $y$-direction as in the $x$-direction).

## 4. Verification and Consistency Checks
1. **Part 1 (Shifted Ellipse) Verification:**
   Let us substitute all four vertices into the original equation $4x^2 + 9y^2 - 8x + 36y + 4 = 0$:
   - $(1, 0)$: $4(1)^2 + 9(0)^2 - 8(1) + 36(0) + 4 = 4 - 8 + 4 = 0$ (holds).
   - $(1, -4)$: $4(1)^2 + 9(-4)^2 - 8(1) + 36(-4) + 4 = 4 + 144 - 8 - 144 + 4 = 0$ (holds).
   - $(4, -2)$: $4(16) + 9(4) - 8(4) + 36(-2) + 4 = 64 + 36 - 32 - 72 + 4 = 104 - 104 = 0$ (holds).
   - $(-2, -2)$: $4(4) + 9(4) - 8(-2) + 36(-2) + 4 = 16 + 36 + 16 - 72 + 4 = 72 - 72 = 0$ (holds).

2. **Part 2 (Elliptic Cone) Verification:**
   At height $z = 2$, our cross-section formula gives the ellipse $\frac{x^2}{1^2} + \frac{y^2}{2^2} = 1$, with vertices $(\pm 1, 0, 2)$ and $(0, \pm 2, 2)$:
   - Substituting $(1, 0, 2)$ into $4x^2 + y^2 = z^2$: $4(1)^2 + 0^2 = 4 = 2^2$ (holds).
   - Substituting $(0, 2, 2)$ into $4x^2 + y^2 = z^2$: $4(0)^2 + 2^2 = 4 = 2^2$ (holds).
